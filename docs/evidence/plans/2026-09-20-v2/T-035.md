# T-035 — Decide whether audio is a feature or a by-product

> **DECIDED 2026-09-20 — `DEC-0022`: the audio is kept.** Option A, audio is a feature. The
> recording is what makes *Transcribe again* possible, and re-running a bad decode on a better
> model is the product's whole answer to a weak on-device model.
>
> **The consequence is that `T-024` becomes obligatory, not optional**, and grows a player: the
> interface must say that audio is stored, show how much, and allow deleting it per note and in
> bulk. The default stays *keep everything* — a retention defaulting to deletion would destroy
> months of recordings on the first launch after the update. This task's own remaining work is the
> one-line statement in the module doc; the rest is `T-024`.


- **Severity:** High
- **Work package:** WP-6 (product decisions)
- **Closes:** G-12, G-02
- **Depends on:** nothing to decide. **T-023 (export) must ship before any retention sweep deletes
  a byte.** The answer sets T-024's scope.
- **Needs a headset:** no to decide; yes to verify either answer (`du -sh` on the vault)
- **Estimated blast radius:** Option A — 2 files, 1 new control, 2 strings, 1 test. Option B —
  3 files, 1 setting, 2 strings, 1 sweep with a fake-clock test.

> **This is the operator's decision, not the developer's.** Both answers are defensible and they
> lead to different products. What is **not** defensible is the current state, which is neither:
> the audio is kept forever, cannot be played, cannot be deleted on its own, is not counted
> anywhere, and is not mentioned by a single string in the app.

## What is wrong, in the person's terms

The person dictates into a headset for months believing they are keeping text. What they are
actually keeping is an audio archive of everything they have ever said: one `.wav` per dictation,
named `<uuid>.wav`, sitting beside the note in the vault. There is no player, no listing, no size,
no way to delete a recording while keeping its note, and no string anywhere in the interface that
uses the word *audio* or *recording* about the vault.

The only string about the vault is `app/src/main/res/values/strings.xml:86`:

> Every note is mirrored there as Markdown. Tap the path to copy it.

Markdown is about 0.1% of what is stored there.

## Where it is

**What writes it.** `feature-stt/src/main/kotlin/.../WavWriter.kt:11-34` — raw 16 kHz, 16-bit,
mono PCM with a WAV header. `app/.../VoiceViewModel.kt:116-118` writes it into the vault root,
`feature-vault/.../Vault.kt:58`, which is `File(appContext.filesDir, "vault")` (`Graph.kt:47`).

**The size, derived rather than asserted.** 16 000 samples/s × 2 bytes × 1 channel = **32 kB/s**, so
a 30-second dictation is **960 kB**. At the operator's plausible rate of twenty dictations a day:

| | |
|---|---|
| per dictation (30 s) | ~0.96 MB |
| per day (20) | ~19 MB |
| per week | ~134 MB |
| per year | **~7 GB** |

None of it visible, reducible or reachable from inside the app (G-01 — the vault is app-private
internal storage with no SAF picker, no `FileProvider` and no share intent).

**What reads it.** One thing: *Transcribe again* (`TodayScreen.kt:405-440`). Nothing else in the
tree opens a `.wav` after it is written.

**What deletes it.** Two things, complete (G-13): deleting the note it belongs to
(`Vault.kt:110-131` removes `<id>.md` **and** `<id>.wav`), and `VoiceViewModel.cancel()` deleting
the scratch file of a recording that was never committed. There is no retention, no bulk delete,
no way to drop a recording and keep its note.

**What the product promised.** `docs/ux/foundation.md` JTBD-01 names the anxiety directly: *a
transcript I cannot trust is worse than no note.* The only repair the build offers is running a
different model and hoping. A person who cannot tell whether "тридцать" was "тринадцать" has to
remember what they said — which is the thing the app exists to stop them having to do.

## The two answers

These are one decision with two faces. **Doing neither is the only wrong answer**, and it is what
ships today.

### Option A — audio is a feature

The recording is a first-class artefact the person can hear.

**What ships:**

1. **A play control on the note row** next to *Transcribe again* — `MediaPlayer` over the WAV, a
   play/stop toggle, a duration. Twenty to forty lines.
2. **A size disclosure in Settings** — "N recordings · X MB" beside the vault path
   (`SettingsScreen.kt:256-275`).
3. **Per-recording delete** — *Delete recording*, keeping the note and the transcript. This is the
   control G-13 says does not exist, and it is what makes the archive manageable by hand.
4. **A string that names the audio** — `settings_vault_hint` (`strings.xml:86`) currently names
   only Markdown. This is T-024's and T-025's ground; this task decides that it must say *audio*.

**Cost:** ~2 days including tests and the Settings arithmetic.

**What it buys:** 99.9% of the stored bytes stop being a liability and become the feature that
justifies them. JTBD-01's repair path becomes "listen to what I said" instead of "run a bigger
model and hope".

**What it costs, said plainly:** growth stays unbounded. 7 GB a year, on a 128 GB headset that also
holds up to 1.4 GB of whisper models (G-08). A player makes the archive worth having; it does not
make it finite. **If A is chosen, retention is still needed** — it just becomes a setting the
person chooses rather than a default that protects them.

### Option B — audio is a by-product

The recording exists to serve *Transcribe again*, and nothing else. It is kept for a bounded
window and then swept.

**What ships:**

1. **A retention setting with a real default** — *Keep recordings for 30 days* — and a sweep on
   `Graph.scope`.
2. **A string that says what the window means**, because it has a consequence a person must be
   able to predict: *Transcribe again* stops working on a note older than the window. That is a
   product statement, not an implementation note, and it belongs on the Settings row and in the
   scenario.
3. **The size disclosure** in Settings (same as A.2) — the number is what makes the retention
   setting meaningful.
4. **Per-recording delete** (same as A.3) — cheap, and the person who wants one gone now should not
   have to wait thirty days.

**Cost:** ~1 day. The sweep is the only new mechanism and it is testable without a device.

**What it buys:** growth becomes bounded and disclosed. Thirty days at twenty dictations a day is
~580 MB steady-state, not 7 GB and rising.

**What it costs, said plainly:** the audio is gone, permanently, and the person cannot get it back.
`Transcribe again` on an old note becomes unavailable — the control must disappear from the row
rather than fail, or it is A-03's defect again in a different place.

### Not an option, but an amplifier for either

**Store Opus rather than raw PCM.** A 30-second dictation at 24 kbps Opus is ~90 kB against
~960 kB — a **10.7×** reduction — and whisper only ever needs 16 kHz PCM at transcription time, so
the decode is at the point of use. This changes the arithmetic of both options by an order of
magnitude; it does not answer the question. **It is separate work and should be a separate row**,
because it touches `WavWriter`, the JNI feed path and *Transcribe again* at once, and neither
option is blocked on it.

## Recommendation

**Option B — a by-product, with a 30-day default, plus the per-recording delete and the size line
from A.**

Three reasons:

1. **Retention is the only option that bounds the problem.** A player makes the bytes valuable; it
   does not stop them accumulating. If A is chosen, retention still has to be built, so B is a
   strict subset of the work A eventually needs.
2. **The product cannot yet create a text note** (A-01, T-028). Adding a media player to a surface
   with that hole in it spends the next build on the wrong thing.
3. **The privacy property is the urgent half.** G-02 is a Blocker because the product acquired an
   undisclosed audio archive of everything the person has ever said. Retention plus disclosure
   closes that. A player alone does not — it makes the archive audible, which is not the same as
   making it bounded or chosen.

**If the operator wants playback, it is Option A and the retention still ships** — as a setting
defaulting to *Keep forever*, with the size line loud enough that the choice is informed.

**The ordering constraint is absolute either way: T-023 (export) ships before the first sweep
runs.** A retention sweep that deletes audio a person could not first copy off the device is the
same failure as H-23, arriving on a timer instead of on an `adb uninstall`.

## Decomposition

**Common to both (this is T-024's body; this task fixes its scope):**

1. `DEC` row stating the answer and its trade-off clause. Read `docs/DECISIONS.md:7` for the free
   id; it says the register's next free id today and reading it is not reserving it.
2. `SettingsScreen.kt` — a vault line that names audio and counts it: recordings and megabytes,
   computed off the main thread (T-019's dispatcher rules apply).
3. `strings.xml:86` — `settings_vault_hint` gains the audio. This is a user-facing string, so it
   goes through the copywriting route if T-034 answers B1 and through a recorded refusal otherwise.
4. *Delete recording* on the note row, leaving the note and the transcript. It must remove
   `audioPath` from the row in the same transaction, or A-02's dangling-pointer defect returns from
   a different direction.

**If B, additionally:**

5. A retention setting (`SecureSettings` is for secrets; this is a plain preference) with the
   default and the vocabulary written down.
6. A sweep on `Graph.scope`, injected with a clock so it is testable. It must refuse to run if the
   export of T-023 has never been performed **or** must be disclosed loudly enough that this is not
   needed — the operator decides which, and the DEC row says.
7. The row's *Transcribe again* control disappears when the audio is gone. Not disabled, not
   failing: absent, exactly as T-005 argues a Discard that discards nothing is a control that
   teaches confusion.

**If A, additionally:**

5. Play/stop on the row over `MediaPlayer`, released on dispose, off the main thread for the open.
6. A duration on the row, read from the WAV header rather than by decoding.

## Tests

| Test | File | Asserts |
|---|---|---|
| `the vault reports its recordings and its bytes` | `feature-vault/src/test/.../VaultSizeTest.kt` | count and total over a fixture tree; an empty vault reports `0 · 0 MB`, not blank |
| `deleting a recording keeps the note and clears audioPath` | `app/src/test/.../NotesViewModelTest.kt` | the row survives, the file is gone, `audioPath == null` |
| **B:** `the sweep removes recordings older than the window and nothing else` | `feature-vault/src/test/.../RetentionTest.kt`, fake clock | a 31-day-old `.wav` goes; a 29-day-old one stays; **no `.md` is ever touched** |
| **B:** `a note whose recording was swept offers no Transcribe again` | `app/src/test/.../NotesViewModelTest.kt` | the control is absent, not failing |
| **A:** `play opens the recording and releases it` | instrumented | `MediaPlayer` is released on dispose; no second instance |

The `.md` clause in the retention test is the one that must be watched failing first. A sweep that
can reach a Markdown file is a vault-destroying bug with a timer on it.

## Blast radius, and what NOT to touch

- **Touches:** `SettingsScreen.kt`, `Vault.kt` (a size query, and under B a sweep), `TodayScreen.kt`
  (one row control), two or three strings, one `DEC` row.
- **Do not touch:** `WavWriter`'s format. Whisper needs 16 kHz PCM at transcription time, and the
  compression question is a separate row (see *amplifier* above). Changing the format here turns a
  product decision into a native-audio change with the JNI feed path in scope.
- **Do not touch:** `VoiceViewModel.cancel()`'s deletion of the scratch WAV — T-005 depends on that
  behaviour staying exactly as it is, and has a test asserting it.
- **Do not touch:** `Vault.remove()`'s month-folder fallback (`Vault.kt:110-131`, G-25) — it is a
  known Medium with its own row, and a retention sweep must not be the change that alters deletion
  semantics for notes at the same time.
- **Watch for:** the size query walks the vault. On a year-old vault that is thousands of files;
  it must not run on the main thread and must not run on every recomposition. T-019 owns the
  dispatcher rule, and this is a place it applies.
