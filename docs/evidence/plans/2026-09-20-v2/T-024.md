# T-024 — The voice archive is disclosed, bounded and removable

> **Scope settled by `DEC-0022` (`T-035`): option A — the audio is a feature and is kept.** This
> task therefore implements *disclosure and management*, not deletion by policy: count it, say it,
> play it, remove it per note and in bulk, and leave the default at *keep everything*. Option B's
> retention sweep exists but defaults to deleting nothing, and `T-023`'s export had to ship before
> it could delete a byte. Shipped as `DEC-0038` and `DEC-0039`.

- **Severity:** Blocker
- **Work package:** WP-4 (data: search, storage, export)
- **Closes:** G-02, G-16, G-12
- **Depends on:** T-023 (an export must exist before the app offers to delete things in bulk)
- **Needs a headset:** yes for one measurement (`du -sh` against what Settings claims)
- **Estimated blast radius:** 1 new file in `:feature-vault`, `Vault` gains one method,
  `SettingsScreen`/`SettingsViewModel` gain a block, `NotesViewModel` gains one action, ~8 strings,
  2 test files

## What is wrong, in the person's terms

Every dictation writes a `.wav` of the person's voice into the vault and keeps it forever. **No
string anywhere in the product mentions that audio exists.** The one vault sentence is
`settings_vault_hint` (`app/src/main/res/values/strings.xml:86`): "Every note is mirrored there as
Markdown. Tap the path to copy it."

Markdown is roughly 0.1% of what is stored. The audio is the rest.

Someone dictates private material into a headset for months believing they are keeping text. What
they are actually keeping is an audio archive of everything they have ever said that they cannot
hear, cannot list, cannot measure, cannot reach, and can only remove by deleting the note it belongs
to — one row at a time, with an undo buffer of exactly one.

That is a privacy property the product acquired without stating it.

## The arithmetic, from the code

`feature-stt/.../WavWriter.kt:11-34` writes 16-bit mono PCM at 16 kHz: `sampleRate * 2` bytes per
second = **32,000 B/s**, plus a 44-byte header.

| Span | Bytes |
|---|---|
| 30 s dictation | 960,044 B ≈ **0.96 MB** |
| 20 × 30 s per day | **19.2 MB/day** |
| one week | **134 MB** |
| one year | **7.0 GB** |

Beside it: the models are up to 1.4 GB (G-08) and there is no retention of any kind (G-13). On a
128 GB headset this is not a wall next month, which is exactly why it will be ignored until it is
one — and when it is, **the product has no number anywhere** to diagnose it with. Not in Settings,
not in the docs, not in a decision.

## Where it is

- `feature-vault/.../Vault.kt:58` — `audioPathFor(note) = File(pathFor(note).parentFile, "${note.id}.wav")`.
  The recording lives beside the Markdown, in `filesDir/vault/notes/yyyy/MM/`.
- `feature-vault/.../Vault.kt:110-131` — `remove(id, createdAt)` is the **only** thing that deletes a
  stored recording, and it deletes the note's Markdown in the same call. There is no way to separate
  the two.
- `app/.../NotesViewModel.kt:243-254` — `createVoiceNote` adopts the scratch WAV into the vault on
  every dictation. Unconditional.
- `app/.../TodayScreen.kt:405-440` — the row's only use of `note.audioPath` is to decide whether to
  show *Transcribe again*. There is no player, no duration, no size.
- `app/.../SettingsScreen.kt:255-275` — the whole Vault section: a path, an out-of-sync count, one
  hint sentence.
- `grep -rn "audio\|recording\|wav" app/src/main/res core-common/src/main/res` — the word never
  reaches a string the person reads.

## What this task decides, and what it defers to T-035

**T-035 decides whether audio is a feature or a by-product** (G-12, G-02). The two possible answers
are "build a player, and the stored bytes earn their keep" and "stop keeping the audio once the
transcript is good". They are the same decision seen from two ends.

This task **assumes neither** and is correct under both:

| | This task ships | T-035 may later |
|---|---|---|
| Disclosure and the number | yes, unconditionally — true under either answer | — |
| Per-note *Delete recording* | yes — needed under either answer | — |
| Bulk delete by age | yes, as a **mechanism** with a default of *keep everything* | change the default |
| A player | **no** | add one on the row; the *Delete recording* control this task adds is where it would sit |
| Opus/FLAC instead of raw PCM (G-16's 10.7× reduction) | **no** | separate task; it changes what `WhisperEngine` is handed at re-transcription time |

If T-035 lands first and says "cut the audio", this task shrinks to its disclosure half and the
retention default becomes *nothing is kept*. Nothing here would have to be undone.

## The decision, and what was weighed

**Chosen: three things, in this order of importance — say it, size it, let them remove it. A
retention sweep exists but defaults to off.**

**Alternatives considered:**

- *A retention default of "keep 30 days", as G-02 proposes.* **Rejected as a default, kept as an
  option.** It is the right engineering answer and the wrong thing to ship without asking: the
  operator has months of dictations on a headset right now, and the first launch after this change
  would delete them. A destructive default that fires before the person has read the screen that
  announces it is the same class of failure as the *Retry* button in T-005. Ship the mechanism with
  *Keep everything* selected, put the four options in front of them, and let them choose. Record the
  choice as a DEC.
- *Delete the recording automatically once a transcript exists.* Rejected: it removes the only thing
  that makes *Transcribe again* possible (`TodayScreen.kt:406`), which is the product's entire
  repair path for a wrong transcript (JTBD-01: "a transcript I cannot trust is worse than no note").
- *Show the size on the Today screen.* Rejected: it is a number a person needs twice a year, and
  Today is the screen T-030 is trying to get under 360 dp. Settings is where a storage number
  belongs.
- *Compute the size on every Settings open, synchronously.* Rejected: walking a 7 GB tree on the
  main thread is T-019's exact prohibition. It runs on the IO dispatcher, into state, with the row
  showing a placeholder until it lands.
- *A "delete all recordings" button and nothing else.* Rejected: an all-or-nothing control for the
  most sensitive thing stored is not a choice, it is a cliff. Age-based removal lets someone keep
  this month and drop last year.

## Decomposition

**Step 1 — write the failing tests first.** Extend
`feature-vault/src/test/kotlin/ai/passioncode/fabricvr/vault/VaultAudioTest.kt`:

1. `the vault reports how many recordings it holds and how many bytes` — write three wavs of known
   size across two month folders, assert `audioUsage()` returns `count = 3` and the exact byte sum.
2. `removing a recording leaves the note's markdown alone` — assert the `.wav` is gone and the
   `.md` still parses.
3. `removing recordings older than a cutoff keeps the newer ones` — three wavs with set
   `lastModified`, one cutoff, assert exactly the expected one survives.
4. `a note with no recording is unaffected by the sweep` — no exception, count 0.

Run them. **Watch all four fail** — `Vault` has no such methods.

Then in `app/src/test/.../NotesViewModelTest.kt`:

5. `deleting a recording clears the note's audioPath` — assert the stored `Note.audioPath` is
   `null` afterwards, **and that the note itself is still in the repository**. Without the second
   half this test passes for a `delete()` that removes everything.

**Step 2 — `Vault` gains two methods** (`feature-vault/.../Vault.kt`, the interface at :18-38):

```kotlin
/** How much of the vault is recordings. Walks the tree, so it is a suspend call on [io]. */
suspend fun audioUsage(): Result<AudioUsage>

/**
 * Deletes the recording and keeps the note. Distinct from [remove], which deletes both: the two
 * were one operation, and "delete the recording" had to mean "delete the note" as a result.
 */
suspend fun removeAudio(note: Note): Result<Unit>

/** Deletes every recording last modified before [cutoff]. Returns what went. */
suspend fun sweepAudio(cutoff: Long): Result<AudioUsage>
```

with `data class AudioUsage(val count: Int, val bytes: Long)`. Implement in `FileVault` using the
same `withContext(io)` + `runCatchingCancellable` + `VaultException(AppError.Storage(...))` shape as
`write` (`Vault.kt:83-103`) so failures already have words.

**Step 3 — clearing the note's pointer.** `removeAudio` deletes the file; the row in the database
still carries `audioPath`. `NotesViewModel` gains:

```kotlin
/** The recording goes; the note, its text and its transcript stay. */
fun deleteRecording(note: Note) { … vault.removeAudio(note) … repository.upsert(note.copy(audioPath = null)) … }
```

Order matters: file first, then the row. If the row is cleared first and the delete fails, the note
points at nothing while the bytes stay — the worst of both.

**The sweep must do the same for every note it touches.** A sweep that deletes files and leaves
`audioPath` populated turns every swept note into a *Transcribe again* button that fails. Give the
sweep the repository, or have it return the ids it cleared and let the caller upsert them; either
way there is a test for it (test 6 below).

**Step 4 — Settings.** Under the existing *Vault* heading (`SettingsScreen.kt:256`), after the path
and the out-of-sync row, a **Recordings** block:

- `settings_audio_usage` — «%1$d recordings · %2$s» with the size formatted by
  `android.text.format.Formatter.formatShortFileSize`, so the unit is the platform's and the
  translation gets it free;
- `settings_audio_explain` — the sentence that does not exist today: "Every dictation keeps its
  recording so it can be transcribed again. Recordings are the largest thing this app stores.";
- a *Delete recordings older than…* control: four `FilterChip`s — *Keep everything* (default) /
  *90 days* / *30 days* / *7 days* — and a *Delete now* button that states the count and size it is
  about to remove **before** it removes it.
- If the operator chooses an age, it is stored in `SecureSettings` and the sweep runs once on
  `Graph.scope` at app start. One new key; follow `SecureSettings`'s existing `KEY_*` naming.

**Step 5 — the note row.** In `NoteRow` (`TodayScreen.kt:405-440`), inside the existing
`if (note.audioPath != null)` block, beside *Transcribe again*: an `OutlinedButton`
*Delete recording* (`action_delete_recording`). Use whatever shared control T-030 introduces for
the 72 dp floor (B-18); do not add a bare `OutlinedButton` if that task has already landed.

**Step 6 — the strings.** New in `app/src/main/res/values/strings.xml`:

```xml
<string name="label_recordings">Recordings</string>
<string name="settings_audio_usage">%1$d recordings · %2$s</string>
<string name="settings_audio_explain">Every dictation keeps its recording so it can be transcribed again. Recordings are the largest thing this app stores.</string>
<string name="label_audio_retention">Delete recordings older than</string>
<string name="label_retention_keep_all">Keep everything</string>
<string name="label_retention_days">%1$d days</string>
<string name="action_delete_old_recordings">Delete %1$d recordings (%2$s)</string>
<string name="action_delete_recording">Delete recording</string>
<string name="state_recording_deleted">The recording is gone. The note stays.</string>
```

`settings_vault_hint` (`:86`) is now incomplete rather than wrong — it says Markdown and stops.
**Amending it is T-032's**, which owns the whole "every string is true of this build" rule; name it
in the commit so T-032's sweep does not have to rediscover it.

## Correction to the finding

**G-02's proposed fix (3) — "a retention setting with a real default — keep recordings for 30
days"** — is rejected as written, and the rejection is deliberate rather than a scope cut. Shipping
a destructive default deletes months of the operator's recordings on the first launch after the
update, before they have seen the screen that announces the feature. The mechanism ships; the
default is *Keep everything*; the choice is put in front of them once. G-02's *analysis* is right
and its severity is right — it is only the default that is wrong.

**G-12 is not closed by this task in the sense it asks for.** G-12 wants a player ("twenty lines and
it turns 99.9% of the stored bytes from a liability into the feature that justifies them"). This
task closes the half of G-12 that is unconditional — the person can now see the recordings exist,
see what they cost, and remove them — and hands the player to **T-035**, which is the task that
decides whether the bytes should be there at all. Marking G-12 fully closed here would be false.

## Blast radius, and what NOT to touch

- **Touches:** `Vault` interface + `FileVault` (three methods), `NotesViewModel` (one action),
  `SettingsViewModel`/`SettingsScreen` (one block), `TodayScreen.NoteRow` (one button),
  `SecureSettings` (one key), nine strings, two test files.
- **Do not touch:** `WavWriter` — the Opus/FLAC change is G-16's other half and belongs to its own
  task, with `WhisperEngine`'s input in scope. `FileVault.remove` — it is the note-deletion path
  and T-006 (*Undo* restores the note **and** its audio) depends on its current behaviour;
  `removeAudio` is a new method beside it, not a change to it. `createVoiceNote`'s adoption
  (`NotesViewModel.kt:243-254`) — whether to record at all is T-035's.
- **Watch for:** **T-006 and this task collide on one file.** T-006 makes *Undo* restore the
  recording; *Delete recording* deliberately does not offer an undo, because the file is gone and
  there is nothing to restore. Whichever lands second must confirm the other's test still passes,
  and the row that says *Delete recording* must not be mistaken by a reader for one the undo
  covers — if T-006 has landed, the copy for `state_recording_deleted` must not sit next to an
  *Undo* control.
- **Watch for:** the sweep runs on `Graph.scope` at start-up and walks the whole vault. It must not
  race the first `VaultMirror` write, and it must be cancellable.
